## Project Overview

This project involved assembling a functional FM radio from a DIY electronics kit. The build provided hands-on experience with printed circuit boards (PCBs), soldering techniques, electronic components, and basic electrical testing.

Throughout the assembly process, I practiced identifying and installing components, inspecting solder connections, and troubleshooting issues using a digital multimeter.

The completed radio successfully powers on, receives FM radio stations, and operates through its onboard controls.

## Project Objectives

- Develop practical soldering and PCB assembly skills.
- Learn to identify and correctly position electronic components.
- Practice continuity testing with a digital multimeter.
- Troubleshoot and repair assembly-related issues.
- Document the build process and lessons learned.

## Tools & Equipment

The following tools and materials were used during assembly and troubleshooting:

**Temperature-controlled soldering iron** - Used to solder electronic components to the PCB.

**Solder and liquid flux** - Used to create and improve solder connections.

**Solder sucker and desoldering braid** - Used to remove solder and correct connections.

**Electronics assembly instructions** - Used to identify components and their placement on the PCB.

## Troubleshooting & Lessons Learned
### Tactile Switch Alignment

During assembly, one of the tactile switches was positioned incorrectly. I corrected the issue by desoldering the switch, repositioning it, and soldering it back into place.

This provided practical experience with component rework and reinforced the importance of checking component alignment before completing solder connections.

### Soldering Technique & Heat Management

During this project, I used a soldering iron set to approximately 350°C. As a beginner, I initially suspected that this temperature may have been too high, although I later learned that the appropriate temperature depends on the solder, components, and amount of time heat is applied.

I also learned that proper soldering technique involves heating both the component lead and the PCB pad before feeding solder into the joint. Initially, I was unaware of this process and was still developing an understanding of how to achieve consistent solder connections.

These experiences helped me recognize the importance of heat transfer, solder flow, and contact time when working with electronic components.

For future projects, I plan to focus on improving temperature control, heating technique, and solder joint consistency.
